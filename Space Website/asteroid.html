Asteroids, Comets,
and Meteors

From rocky asteroids to icy comets and glowing meteors,
these objects journey through space and play an important role
in shaping our understanding of the universe.

Asteroids

An asteroid is a rocky object that orbits the Sun and is usually found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. Asteroids come in many different sizes, ranging from small rocks to large bodies hundreds of kilometers wide. Studying asteroids helps scientists learn more about the early formation of the solar system.

Comets

A comet is an icy object made of dust, gas, and frozen water that travels around the Sun in a long, curved orbit. When a comet gets close to the Sun, its ice begins to heat up and release gas and dust, forming a glowing cloud called a coma and a bright tail. Comets are considered some of the most ancient objects in the solar system and provide valuable information about its origins.

Meteors

A meteor is the bright streak of light that appears in the sky when a small piece of rock or metal from space enters Earth’s atmosphere. As it moves through the air at high speed, friction causes it to heat up and glow, which is why we can see it from the ground. Meteors are often called “shooting stars,” even though they are not actually stars.
