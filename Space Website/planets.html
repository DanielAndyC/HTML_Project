Introduction to the
Stars & Planets

Currently, we know that there are eight planets
(Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune)
and five dwarf planets (Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris) inside our solar system.

What is a Planet?

A planet is a large celestial body that orbits a star, has enough mass for its gravity
to make it nearly round, and has cleared its orbital path of other significant objects,
according to the International Astronomical Union's (IAU) definition for our Solar System.
This means it must orbit the Sun (for our system), be spherical, and dominate its orbit.

Inner Planets

Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system and the closest one to the Sun. Because it has almost no atmosphere, Mercury experiences extreme temperature changes—very hot during the day and extremely cold at night. A year on Mercury is very short, taking only 88 Earth days to orbit the Sun, but one day on Mercury is very long compared to Earth.

Venus is often called Earth’s “twin” because it is similar in size, but its environment is very different. It has a thick atmosphere filled with carbon dioxide, which traps heat and makes Venus the hottest planet in the solar system. Venus also rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction of most planets, causing the Sun to rise in the west and set in the east.

Earth is the only planet known to support life. It has liquid water, a protective atmosphere, and a suitable distance from the Sun that allows temperatures to remain stable. Earth’s atmosphere and magnetic field protect living organisms from harmful radiation, making it a unique and habitable planet.

Mars is known as the “Red Planet” because of the iron oxide on its surface. Scientists believe Mars once had water, as shown by dried riverbeds and polar ice caps. Although it is cold and has a thin atmosphere, Mars is one of the most explored planets and a potential target for future human missions.

Outer Planets

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system and is famous for its Great Red Spot, a massive storm that has lasted for centuries. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium and has dozens of moons, including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system. Jupiter’s strong gravity helps protect inner planets by pulling in asteroids and comets.

Saturn is best known for its stunning ring system, made of ice and rock particles. Like Jupiter, Saturn is a gas giant composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. It has many moons, including Titan, which has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.

Uranus is an ice giant with a blue-green color caused by methane in its atmosphere. It is unique because it rotates on its side, making its seasons extremely long and unusual. Uranus has faint rings and very cold temperatures, ranking it among the coldest planets in the solar system.

Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun and is known for its deep blue color and powerful winds. It has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching speeds faster than the speed of sound. Neptune is an ice giant with a mysterious and dynamic atmosphere, making it one of the most fascinating planets to study.

Stars

The Sun

Our Sun is a star at the center of the solar system that provides light and heat necessary for life on Earth. It is a massive ball of hot gases, mainly hydrogen and helium, that produces energy through nuclear fusion in its core. The Sun’s gravity holds the planets in orbit, and its energy drives weather, climate, and natural processes on our planet.
